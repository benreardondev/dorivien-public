Implementation

Configured around the business, not dropped in blindly.

A practical path from demo to a client-ready deployment.

1. Map the real lead flow

Document where inquiries arrive, who currently responds, the services offered, the service area, business hours, booking rules, after-hours process, escalation contacts, and the systems the team already uses.

2. Build the approved business profile

Dorivien's production rules are intended to live server-side. The profile can contain approved services, hours, service area, booking policy, escalation rules, and other information the assistant is permitted to use. Customer messages do not get to redefine those rules.

3. Test before launch

Normal service requests, incomplete messages, urgent no-heat/no-cooling situations, quote requests, unsupported questions, adversarial prompts, and safety scenarios should all be tested before customers see the deployment.

4. Choose the handoff

A deployment can remain intake-only or send a completed lead to a client-approved webhook or automation. A handoff should preserve the structured lead record and leave appointment confirmation with the business unless a real booking integration is deliberately configured.

5. Restrict the client widget

The public demo can be embedded broadly. A production widget should be restricted to the client's approved website origin and tested on desktop and mobile.

6. Launch narrowly, then expand

Start with one clear problem such as website inquiry response. Add missed-call SMS, CRM routing, quote follow-up, or other workflows only after the first workflow is reliable and useful.

Dorivien's public demo is non-transactional. A real client deployment requires business-specific configuration, testing, and sign-off.
